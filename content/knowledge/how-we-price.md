# How we arrive at a price

The ranges on the pricing page are real. This is what moves a project inside
one, so nobody has to guess.

## The two things that actually drive cost

**How many systems we have to touch.** One clean system with an API sits at the
bottom of a range. Four systems, one of which is a spreadsheet a person
maintains by hand, sits at the top. Every integration is a place where things
can differ from the documentation.

**The state of the data inside them.** Duplicate customer records, three
spellings of the same product, order statuses that mean different things in
different departments. This is where most AI projects quietly fail, and it is
why we do the data audit in week one rather than at the end.

## What does not change the price

- How large your company is. We do not price by what we think you can afford.
- How urgent it is. We cannot buy time with money, and pretending otherwise
  produces a worse system.
- Whether you are the first client in your industry. Later clients benefit from
  reusable modules; we do not charge the first one a premium for the privilege.

## When we tell you the number

In the written build memo, after the first call. Before you commit to anything.
If the data audit in week one changes our view, we tell you immediately rather
than at the invoice.

## Payment

Staged against delivery, not against the calendar. A pilot is billed on the
prototype; a production build is staged across the audit, the working system
and the handover.
