# What we will not build

Telling a prospect not to buy is the cheapest trust we will ever purchase, so
this list is public and Sakha is expected to use it.

## We will decline, or tell you to fix something first

**There is no system to read from.** If the answers live in one person's head
and nowhere else, an agent has nothing to retrieve. Write the answers down
first — that is a week of work and it does not need us.

**There is no CRM and no pipeline.** A follow-up agent cannot chase a pipeline
that does not exist. Get a CRM in place, use it for a month, then talk to us.

**The volume does not justify it.** Below roughly 200 repetitive interactions a
month, the arithmetic rarely works. We will say so.

**You want a demo, not a system.** We do not build proofs of concept that
nobody intends to put into production. That is what the industry already does
too much of, and it is the gap we exist to close.

**The policies contradict each other.** An agent applies a rule consistently,
which means it will surface every place where two of your rules disagree. That
is useful, but it is a policy project before it is an AI project.

**It needs to be right every single time, with no human anywhere.** Some
processes genuinely cannot tolerate an exception path. If yours is one, say so
early and we will tell you honestly whether this technology is ready for it.

## We will not do these at all

- Anything that impersonates a real person without disclosure.
- Anything designed to mislead the person on the other end of the conversation.
- Scraping data someone has asked not to be scraped.
- Agents that make consequential decisions about people — hiring, credit,
  medical triage — without a human making the final call.

## What happens when we decline

You still get the written build memo. It will say what we would not build and,
where we can, what to do instead. It costs us the occasional engagement. It has
never cost us a relationship.
